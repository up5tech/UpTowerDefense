### Diagnosis of the failing chain test (expensive to re-derive)

The implementation is correct; the **test expectation rests on a false
geometric premise**. The scene parks grunts at route distances
`[300, 200, 400, 150]` and claims the one at 400 is outside the tower's
220-unit range. On `testMap` the route turns upward at x=300, so route 400 is
world `(300, 400)` — only ~126 units from pad_a `(180, 360)`, well inside
range, while route 300 is the corner `(300, 500)` at ~184 units. Targeting
picks the leader (furthest along route among those in range), so the tower
targets instance 2, and the chain runs 2 → 0 → 1, giving
`{0: 88, 1: 94, 2: 76, 3: 100}` instead of the expected
`{0: 76, 1: 88, 2: 100, 3: 94}`.

A fix is not a one-liner: an *exact* distance tie only survives enemy movement
if the tied candidates and the chain origin share one straight route segment.
On the shared `testMap` the tie partner beyond a corner is always at a higher
route distance, so it becomes the leader instead. The scene needs either a
straight-route map fixture of its own or a re-thought layout.

### Fixed the failing chain tie-break test

The implementation was right; the test's premise was wrong. Two independent
bugs in the old scene:

1. It assumed route distance 400 was outside the tower's 220-unit range. On
   `testMap` the route turns upward at x=300, so route 400 is world
   `(300, 400)` — about 126 units from pad_a, well inside range — and the
   leading-enemy rule made the tower target it.
2. Even with the right target, a tie only exists at `Δ = 0`. Enemies move
   before combat in every frame (`advance` moves them, then runs combat), so
   by the time the chain resolves the two candidates sit at different float
   values and the exact-equality tie branch never fires.

Fix (test-only, no production change):

- `test/helpers/content_fixtures.dart`: added `testStationaryGrunt`
  (`speed: 0`) and `stationaryEnemyCatalog`. A field that does not move
  keeps the measured distance exact.
- `test/unit/tower_chain_test.dart`: added a test-local `_tieMap` whose route
  turns in exact hundred-unit steps, so a parked enemy stands on an exact
  waypoint (`t = 1.0` exactly, no interpolation error) and the two
  candidates around the corner are exactly 100 units apart — `10000.0` on
  both sides, bitwise identical. Its pad sits below the corner, which keeps
  the far candidate out of the tower's range so the corner is the target.
  The tie-break test now uses `_tieScene`; the other four chain tests keep
  the moving field, so the moving case stays covered.

Verified by mutation: flipping `<` to `>` in the tie-break comparison makes
the test fail with the chain order inverted, so the test really exercises
the rule rather than the list order.
